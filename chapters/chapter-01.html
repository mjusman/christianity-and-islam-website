Chapter 1: The Meaning of Peace in Christianity and Islam

Advertisement

Introduction

Peace is one of humanity's deepest aspirations. Every person desires to live in safety, dignity, justice, and harmony with others. Families flourish where peace is present, communities prosper when conflicts are resolved peacefully, and nations become stronger when justice replaces violence. Throughout history, however, misunderstandings, injustice, prejudice, and religious conflict have often threatened this peace.

Christianity and Islam, despite their important theological differences, both place great value on peace. Their sacred scriptures encourage believers to pursue reconciliation, show mercy, practice justice, care for their neighbors, and live responsibly before God. While neither faith promises a world entirely free from conflict, both teach that peace should be actively pursued through righteousness, compassion, and respect for human dignity. This chapter explores the meaning of peace in the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible, showing that peace is more than the absence of war. It is a way of living that reflects obedience to God, justice toward others, and hope for a better future.

The Meaning of Peace in the Holy Qur'an

The Arabic word "Salām" means peace, safety, security, and well-being. It is closely related to the word "Islam," which carries the meaning of submission to God. One of the Beautiful Names of Allah is As-Salām— "The Source of Peace."

1. Allah Invites Humanity to Peace

Surah Yunus (10:25)

"Allah invites to the Home of Peace and guides whom He wills to a straight path."

This verse presents peace as one of God's invitations to humanity. The ultimate destination of believers is described as a home of everlasting peace.

2. Peace Is the Preferred Path

Surah Al-Anfal (8:61)

"If they incline toward peace, then incline toward it also and put your trust in Allah."

Whenever peaceful reconciliation is possible without sacrificing justice, believers are encouraged to accept it.

3. Greeting Others with Peace

Surah An-Nur (24:61)

The Qur'an encourages believers to greet one another with words of peace. The Islamic greeting, "As-salāmu ʿalaykum" ("Peace be upon you"), is more than a social custom. It is a daily prayer for another person's safety, well-being, and God's blessings.

4. Reconciliation Between Believers

Surah Al-Hujurat (49:9-10)

The Qur'an commands believers to reconcile those who are in conflict and reminds them that they are brothers and sisters in faith.

Peacemaking is therefore not optional but a religious responsibility.

The Meaning of Peace in the Holy Bible

The Hebrew word "Shalom" means peace, completeness, wholeness, harmony, and well-being. Like the Arabic Salām, it describes a condition in which individuals and communities flourish under God's blessing. The New Testament continues this theme using the Greek word "Eirēnē,"

referring to peace with God and peace among people.

1. Blessed Are the Peacemakers

Matthew 5:9

Jesus teaches: "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called children of God."

This teaching places peacemaking at the heart of faithful living.

2. Live Peacefully with Everyone

Romans 12:18

The Apostle Paul writes: "If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone."

Believers are encouraged to pursue peaceful relationships wherever possible.

3. Christ's Greeting of Peace

Following His resurrection, Jesus repeatedly greeted His disciples with the words: "Peace be with you." (John 20:19–21) This greeting reflects the importance of peace as a gift from God and as a defining characteristic of Christian fellowship.

4. Seek Peace and Pursue It

Psalm 34:14

The Psalmist writes: "Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it."

Peace is not passive. It requires deliberate effort, moral courage, and perseverance.

Shared Principles of Peace

Although Christianity and Islam differ in theology, they share several important ethical principles concerning peace.

Both traditions teach that: Peace originates with God.

 Human life possesses inherent dignity.

 Justice is essential for lasting peace.

 Mercy strengthens communities.

 Forgiveness promotes reconciliation.

 Love for one's neighbor is a moral duty.

 Believers should avoid unnecessary hostility.

 Dialogue is preferable to hatred.

 Reconciliation is better than prolonged conflict.

 These shared principles provide a strong foundation for cooperation between Christians and Muslims in promoting peaceful societies.

Peace Begins Within the Individual

Both scriptures teach that lasting peace in society begins with the character of individuals.

A peaceful person strives to cultivate: Faithfulness to God.

 Honesty.

 Patience.

 Humility.

 Self-control.

 Compassion.

 Forgiveness.

 Respect for others.

 Inner peace enables people to respond wisely during conflict and to resist anger, prejudice, and revenge.

Peace Within Families

Families are the first schools of peace.

Parents who model kindness, justice, forgiveness, and mutual respect help children develop values that benefit society throughout their lives. Both the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible encourage spouses to live with love, faithfulness, patience, and compassion, creating homes where peace can flourish.

Peace Within Communities

Communities become peaceful when individuals choose cooperation over hostility.

Christians and Muslims can strengthen community life by: Respecting one another's dignity.

 Protecting places of worship.

 Helping the poor.

 Supporting education.

 Caring for the sick.

 Defending justice.

 Resolving disputes peacefully.

 Working together during times of crisis.

 Shared acts of service often build trust more effectively than words alone.

Peace Among Nations

The pursuit of peace extends beyond individuals and communities to relationships between nations.

Governments bear the responsibility to: Protect human rights.

 Prevent unnecessary violence.

 Resolve disputes peacefully.

 Promote justice.

 Respect international agreements.

 Cooperate in humanitarian efforts.

 Religious communities likewise have an important role in encouraging reconciliation rather than hostility.

Peace Requires Justice

Neither the Holy Qur'an nor the Holy Bible presents peace as the mere absence of conflict.

Peace without justice is unstable.

When oppression, corruption, discrimination, or violence are tolerated, genuine peace cannot endure. Lasting peace requires fairness, accountability, and protection of the rights of every individual.

Justice and peace therefore strengthen one another.

Building a Culture of Peace

Peace becomes a lasting reality when it is practiced consistently. Every person contributes to peace by: Speaking truthfully.

 Listening respectfully.

 Rejecting prejudice.

 Helping those in need.

 Resolving disagreements peacefully.

 Respecting religious differences.

 Defending the dignity of others.

 Choosing forgiveness over revenge.

 Small acts of kindness often become the foundation of stronger families, healthier communities, and more peaceful nations.

Reflection

The desire for peace is shared by people of every nation and every faith. While Christians and Muslims hold different theological convictions, both traditions call believers to become instruments of peace through justice, mercy, compassion, and faithful service to God.

Peace is not achieved simply by avoiding conflict. It is built through daily choices to respect others, seek justice, forgive wrongdoing, and cooperate for the common good.

Every believer has the opportunity to become a peacemaker within their family, workplace, community, and nation.

Conclusion

The Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible both affirm that peace is one of God's greatest blessings and one of humanity's highest responsibilities. Peace is expressed through justice, compassion, reconciliation, and respect for the dignity of every person.

Although Christianity and Islam differ on important theological matters, they share enduring moral teachings that encourage believers to seek peace, reject unnecessary violence, and work together for the welfare of society. The journey toward peace begins with the individual, grows within families and communities, and ultimately contributes to a more just and harmonious world.

"Allah invites to the Home of Peace..."

— Qur'an 10:25 "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called children of God."

— Matthew 5:9 When people of faith commit themselves to justice, mercy, and mutual respect, they become living witnesses that peace is not merely an ideal to be admired, but a calling to be lived.

Chapter 11: Jesus Christ (ʿĪsā ibn Maryam): A Shared Figure of Respect

Introduction

Among all the personalities mentioned in the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible, Jesus Christ occupies a unique and honored position. Christians revere Jesus as the central figure of their faith, while Muslims honor him as one of the greatest prophets and messengers of God. Although the two religions differ significantly in their theological understanding of Jesus, both affirm his extraordinary life, his miraculous birth, his moral teachings, and his important role in God's plan.

Because Jesus is deeply respected in both traditions, learning about how each faith views him can become an important bridge for dialogue and mutual understanding. Honest discussion of both shared beliefs and theological differences helps reduce misunderstanding while fostering respect. This chapter examines the place of Jesus in the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible, highlighting shared points of respect while acknowledging the differences that distinguish Christianity and Islam.

Jesus in the Holy Qur'an

Jesus is known in Arabic as ʿĪsā ibn Maryam (Jesus, son of Mary). He is mentioned by name 25 times in the Holy Qur'an, while his mother, Mary (Maryam), is mentioned 34 times, more often than in many other sacred texts. Mary is also the only woman mentioned by name in the Qur'an and has an entire chapter named after her: Surah Maryam (Chapter 19).

The Qur'an presents Jesus as one of God's greatest messengers and honors him with several remarkable titles.

1. The Miraculous Birth of Jesus

Surah Aal 'Imran (3:45-47)

The angel announces to Mary that she will give birth to Jesus by the command of God, even though no man has touched her.

The Qur'an affirms the miraculous virgin birth of Jesus as a sign of God's power.

Surah Maryam (19:16-36)

This chapter provides one of the most detailed Qur'anic accounts of the birth of Jesus and the devotion of his mother, Mary.

2. Jesus as the Messiah

Surah Aal 'Imran (3:45)

Jesus is described as Al-Masīḥ (the Messiah), a title of honor given to him in the Qur'an.

3. Jesus Performed Miracles by God's Permission

Surah Aal 'Imran (3:49)

The Qur'an records that Jesus: Healed the blind.

 Cured those afflicted with leprosy.

 Raised the dead.

 Informed people of hidden matters.

 The Qur'an emphasizes that these miracles occurred by God's permission, demonstrating God's power through His messenger.

4. Jesus Called People to Worship God

Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:72)

The Qur'an presents Jesus as calling people to worship God alone and to live righteous lives.

5. Respect for Jesus Is Part of Islamic Faith

Muslims are required to believe in Jesus as one of God's prophets.

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:285)

The Qur'an teaches that believers should not reject any of God's messengers. Disrespecting Jesus or denying his prophethood is therefore inconsistent with Islamic belief.

Jesus in the Holy Bible

The Holy Bible presents Jesus Christ as the central figure of Christianity. His life, teachings, death, and resurrection form the foundation of Christian faith.

1. The Birth of Jesus

Matthew 1:18-25

The Gospel of Matthew records the miraculous conception of Jesus through the Holy Spirit and the faithfulness of Mary and Joseph.

Luke 1:26-38

The Gospel of Luke describes the angel Gabriel's announcement to Mary that she would bear the promised child.

Both the Bible and the Qur'an affirm the miraculous birth of Jesus.

2. The Teachings of Jesus

Throughout the Gospels, Jesus teaches: Love of God.

 Love of neighbor.

 Mercy.

 Forgiveness.

 Humility.

 Compassion.

 Peace.

 Justice.

 His teachings continue to shape the lives of Christians throughout the world.

3. Miracles of Jesus

The Gospels record many miracles performed by Jesus, including: Healing the sick.

 Giving sight to the blind.

 Raising the dead.

 Feeding large crowds.

 Calming storms.

 These miracles demonstrate God's power and Jesus' ministry.

Shared Beliefs About Jesus

Although Christianity and Islam differ significantly regarding the nature of Jesus, they share several important beliefs.

Both traditions affirm that: Jesus was born to the Virgin Mary.

 Mary is honored as a righteous woman.

 Jesus performed extraordinary miracles.

 Jesus taught righteousness.

 Jesus was sent by God.

 Jesus is the Messiah.

 Jesus should be respected and honored.

 These shared beliefs provide an important foundation for respectful dialogue.

Important Theological Differences

A genuine dialogue requires honesty about differences as well as recognition of common ground.

Christian Belief

Most Christian traditions confess that Jesus is the Son of God, the incarnate Word of God, whose death and resurrection are central to God's plan of salvation.

Islamic Belief

Islam teaches that Jesus is one of the greatest prophets and messengers of God, born miraculously to the Virgin Mary, but not divine. Muslims worship God alone and do not regard Jesus as God or the Son of God in the theological sense affirmed by most Christian traditions.

These differences are profound and remain among the central distinctions between the two religions.

This book does not attempt to resolve these theological questions. Instead, it encourages readers to discuss them respectfully while cooperating in areas where shared moral values promote peace and the common good.

Learning from the Example of Jesus

Regardless of these theological differences, both Muslims and Christians admire many of the same virtues displayed in the life of Jesus. His example encourages believers to practice: Compassion.

 Humility.

 Service.

 Truthfulness.

 Patience.

 Mercy.

 Love for others.

 Faithfulness to God.

 These qualities remain valuable for every society.

Jesus as a Bridge for Dialogue

Because Jesus is deeply respected in both Christianity and Islam, conversations about his life can become opportunities for learning rather than conflict.

Such dialogue should be characterized by: Honesty.

 Respect.

 Careful listening.

 Humility.

 Recognition of genuine differences.

 Appreciation for shared values.

 Respectful dialogue strengthens understanding even when agreement is not reached.

Reflection

Jesus Christ occupies an honored place in both the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible.

Christians and Muslims may understand his identity differently, but both traditions call for respect toward him and toward those who sincerely follow their religious convictions.

Learning about one another's beliefs with honesty and courtesy helps replace misunderstanding with knowledge and prejudice with mutual respect.

Conclusion

The Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible both present Jesus as an extraordinary figure whose life continues to inspire billions of people.

Christians honor him as the central figure of their faith, while Muslims revere him as one of God's greatest prophets and messengers. These theological differences should be discussed with sincerity and respect rather than hostility.

By recognizing both the shared beliefs and the genuine distinctions concerning Jesus, Christians and Muslims can strengthen dialogue, deepen mutual understanding, and work together in promoting justice, compassion, and peace.

"...His name will be the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary..."

— Qur'an 3:45 "For unto you is born this day... a Savior, who is Christ the Lord."

— Luke 2:11 Jesus Christ remains one of the most significant figures in human history. Approaching his life with respect, humility, and a commitment to peaceful dialogue can help Christians and Muslims build stronger relationships while remaining faithful to their own beliefs.

Glossary of Important Terms

Advertisement

Introduction

This glossary provides explanations of important religious, historical, and peacebuilding terms used throughout Christianity and Islam: A Shared Journey Toward Peace.

The purpose of this glossary is to help readers understand key concepts from both Christian and Islamic traditions. Some terms have specific meanings within their own religious contexts, and readers should recognize that different communities may explain certain concepts in different ways. This glossary does not attempt to provide complete theological definitions of every term. Rather, it offers a helpful introduction for readers engaging in interfaith study and dialogue.

A

Abraham (Ibrāhīm)

A major prophet and patriarch respected in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. In Islamic tradition, Prophet Ibrāhīm (Abraham) is regarded as a model of faith, submission to God, and devotion to monotheism.

Allah

The Arabic word for God. Arabic-speaking Christians and Muslims both use the word "Allah" to refer to God. In Islam, Allah is the one Creator, the Most Merciful and Most Compassionate.

Apostle

A person sent with a message or mission. In Christianity, the apostles were the followers of Jesus who were commissioned to spread his teachings. The term is also used more broadly for messengers and representatives.

Āmīn (Amen)

An expression used in both Christianity and Islam after prayers, meaning "O God, accept" or expressing agreement with a prayer.

B

Bible

The sacred scripture of Christianity. It consists of the Old Testament and the New Testament. Christians regard the Bible as a source of divine revelation and guidance.

Biblical Prophets

Individuals in the Bible who conveyed God's messages to humanity. Examples include Abraham, Moses, David, and other prophets recognized in Jewish and Christian traditions.

C

Christianity

A monotheistic religion centered on the life, teachings, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christians follow the teachings of Jesus as presented in the New Testament.

Christ

A title meaning "the Anointed One." It comes from the Greek word Christos, which corresponds to the Hebrew term Messiah.

Compassion

A moral quality involving sympathy, kindness, and a desire to relieve the suffering of others. Compassion is strongly emphasized in both Christian and Islamic teachings.

Covenant

A solemn agreement or relationship. In the Bible, covenants describe important relationships between God and humanity. The concept also appears in discussions of God's promises in Islamic and biblical traditions. D

Dialogue

A respectful conversation between individuals or communities seeking understanding. Interfaith dialogue involves discussions between people of different religious traditions.

Dignity of Human Life

The belief that every human being possesses value and should be treated with respect, justice, and compassion.

E

Ethics

Moral principles that guide human behavior and decisions. Religious ethics are teachings about what is right, just, and good.

Extremism

A way of thinking or acting that rejects moderation and may promote hatred, violence, or intolerance. Religious extremism misuses religious ideas to justify harmful actions.

F

Faith

Trust, belief, and commitment toward God and religious teachings.

Forgiveness

The act of releasing resentment or seeking reconciliation after wrongdoing. Both Christianity and Islam emphasize forgiveness while also recognizing the importance of justice.

Freedom of Religion

The principle that individuals should be able to follow, practice, and express their religious beliefs without unjust persecution or coercion. G

Gospel

A term meaning "good news." In Christianity, it refers especially to the message of Jesus Christ and the books of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John in the New Testament.

Grace

In Christianity, God's undeserved favor and mercy toward humanity. The concept of divine mercy is also strongly emphasized in Islam.

H

Hadith

Reports describing the sayings, actions, approvals, and teachings of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Authentic hadith are an important source of guidance in Islamic scholarship.

Human Dignity

The inherent worth and value of every person. Protecting human dignity is a major theme in peacebuilding and religious ethics.

Humanity

All people collectively, regardless of nationality, ethnicity, culture, or religion.

I

Interfaith Dialogue

Respectful communication and cooperation between people of different religious traditions.

Islam

A monotheistic religion based on belief in Allah and guidance revealed through prophets, including Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as the final prophet according to Islamic belief.

Jesus (ʿĪsā ibn Maryam)

Jesus is a central figure in both Christianity and Islam. Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God and Savior. Muslims honor Jesus (ʿĪsā) as a great prophet, the Messiah, and the son of Mary, while holding different beliefs regarding his nature and crucifixion.

J

Justice

Fairness, righteousness, and the proper treatment of people according to moral principles and law.

Jungle Justice

The practice of individuals or groups taking punishment into their own hands without lawful authority or due process. Both Islamic and Christian teachings emphasize justice through fair judgment rather than uncontrolled revenge.

Jihad

An Arabic word meaning "struggle" or "effort." In Islamic tradition, it can refer to striving for righteousness, resisting wrongdoing, or defending against aggression. It is often misunderstood and should be studied within proper Islamic scholarship and context.

K

Kingdom of God

A biblical expression referring to God's rule and the fulfillment of His purposes. It is an important theme in the teachings of Jesus.

Koran / Qur'an

See Qur'an.

M

Mercy

Compassion, forgiveness, and kindness shown toward others. Allah is frequently described in the Qur'an as Most Merciful, and mercy is a central Christian virtue.

Messiah

A title meaning "Anointed One." Christians identify Jesus as the Messiah. Muslims also refer to Jesus (ʿĪsā) as Al-Masīḥ (the Messiah).

Monotheism

Belief in one God. Christianity and Islam are both traditionally classified as monotheistic religions, although they understand God's nature differently.

Muhammad ﷺ

The Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), regarded in Islam as the final messenger of God and the recipient of the Qur'an. P

Peace

A state of harmony, justice, safety, and reconciliation. In religious traditions, peace includes both personal spiritual peace and peaceful relationships among people.

People of the Book (Ahl al-Kitāb)

An Islamic term referring primarily to Jews and Christians, who are recognized in the Qur'an as communities associated with earlier revealed scriptures.

Prophet

A person chosen by God to deliver guidance and a message to humanity. Q

Qur'an

The holy scripture of Islam, believed by Muslims to be the revealed word of Allah, revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the Angel Gabriel. R

Reconciliation

The process of restoring peaceful relationships after conflict, harm, or division.

Revelation

Divine communication or guidance believed to come from God. Christianity and Islam both emphasize the importance of God's revelation.

Righteousness

Moral goodness and living according to God's guidance.

S

Salvation

Deliverance from sin and spiritual separation from God. Christianity and Islam have different theological understandings of salvation.

Scripture

Sacred writings regarded as containing divine guidance.

Sharia

An Arabic term meaning "path." In Islam, it refers to principles and rulings derived from Islamic sources that guide religious, moral, personal, and social life.

Sunnah

The example, teachings, practices, and traditions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, which Muslims follow as guidance alongside the Qur'an.

T

Tolerance

The willingness to respect people who hold different beliefs or opinions.

Torah

The sacred law and scripture associated with Moses in Jewish tradition. It is also recognized in Islamic tradition as an earlier revelation given to Prophet Mūsā (Moses).

U

Ummah

An Arabic term meaning community. In Islam, it often refers to the worldwide community of Muslims.

Unity

The state of cooperation, harmony, and shared purpose among people. W

Worship

Acts of devotion, prayer, obedience, and reverence directed toward God.

Wisdom

The ability to apply knowledge and understanding with good judgment and righteousness.

Z

Zakat

An obligatory form of charitable giving in Islam, intended to support those in need and promote social responsibility.

Conclusion

Understanding language is an important part of building peace.

Many conflicts arise not only from differences in belief but also from misunderstanding, misinformation, and lack of knowledge about other communities.

By learning the meanings of important terms, Christians, Muslims, and people of all backgrounds can communicate more clearly, reduce prejudice, and develop deeper respect for one another.

Knowledge creates understanding.

Understanding creates trust.

And trust creates a stronger foundation for peace.

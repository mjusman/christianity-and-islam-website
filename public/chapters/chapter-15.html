Chapter 15: Rejecting Religious Extremism

Advertisement

Introduction

Religion has inspired countless acts of compassion, charity, justice, and service throughout history. Yet, history also shows that some individuals and groups have misused religious language to justify hatred, violence, oppression, and the killing of innocent people. Such actions distort the moral teachings of religion and undermine peace.

The purpose of this chapter is not to accuse any particular religion or community. Extremism has appeared in different societies, cultures, and religious traditions throughout history. Christians and Muslims alike are called to reject every form of hatred, fanaticism, and violence that contradicts the authentic moral teachings of their faith.

The Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible call believers to uphold justice, protect innocent life, speak truthfully, and pursue peace. These principles leave no room for violence against innocent people or for the misuse of religion to promote injustice.

Understanding Religious Extremism

Religious extremism occurs when individuals or groups: Use religion to justify violence or hatred.

 Deny the dignity and rights of others.

 Promote intolerance instead of justice.

 Encourage unlawful violence.

 Reject peaceful dialogue.

 Claim that cruelty or oppression is an act of faith.

 Such attitudes contradict the values of mercy, justice, and compassion taught in both the Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible.

The Holy Qur'an Rejects Injustice and Violence

1. The Sanctity of Human Life

Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:32)

The Qur'an teaches: Whoever kills an innocent person unjustly is as though he has killed all of humanity, and whoever saves a life is as though he has saved all of humanity. This verse emphasizes that innocent human life is sacred and must never be treated lightly.

2. Justice Without Hatred

Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:8)

Allah says: "Do not let the hatred of a people prevent you from being just. Be just; that is nearer to righteousness."

Even when disagreements exist, justice must never be abandoned.

3. Diversity Is Part of God's Plan

Surah Al-Hujurat (49:13)

Allah says: "O mankind, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another..."

Human diversity is presented as an opportunity for understanding rather than hostility.

4. Justice and Excellence

Surah An-Nahl (16:90)

Allah commands believers to uphold justice, kindness, and generosity while forbidding oppression and wrongdoing.

These principles stand in direct opposition to extremism.

The Holy Bible Rejects Hatred and Violence

1. Blessed Are the Peacemakers

Matthew 5:9

Jesus teaches: "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called children of God."

Followers of Christ are encouraged to become agents of peace rather than conflict.

2. Love Your Enemies

Matthew 5:44

Jesus says: "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you."

This command challenges believers to overcome hatred with compassion.

3. Live Peacefully With Everyone

Romans 12:18

The Apostle Paul writes: "If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone."

Christians are encouraged to seek peaceful relationships whenever possible.

4. Wisdom From Above

James 3:17-18

The Bible describes godly wisdom as: Pure.

 Peace-loving.

 Gentle.

 Merciful.

 Impartial.

 Sincere.

 Such wisdom produces righteousness and peace, not violence.

Rejecting Fanaticism

Fanaticism often begins when people refuse to listen, refuse to learn, or refuse to recognize the dignity of those who differ from them.

Believers should reject attitudes such as: Religious arrogance.

 Blind hatred.

 Dehumanizing language.

 Calls to unlawful violence.

 Collective blame against entire religious communities.

 False accusations and misinformation.

 Instead, they should pursue humility, wisdom, and respectful dialogue.

The Responsibility of Religious Leaders

Religious leaders play a vital role in preventing extremism by: Teaching authentic religious principles.

 Correcting false interpretations of scripture.

 Encouraging respect for human dignity.

 Condemning violence against innocent people.

 Promoting justice and peaceful coexistence.

 Helping young people resist messages of hatred.

 Responsible leadership strengthens communities and protects society from division.

The Responsibility of Families and Educators

Parents, teachers, and community leaders also have important responsibilities.

Young people should be taught: Respect for every human being.

 Critical thinking.

 The value of peaceful dialogue.

 Responsible use of social media.

 Respect for lawful authority.

 Compassion for people of different backgrounds.

 Education is one of the strongest safeguards against extremism.

Working Together Against Extremism

Christians and Muslims can cooperate to prevent religious extremism by: Promoting accurate religious education.

 Encouraging interfaith dialogue.

 Supporting community peace initiatives.

 Protecting places of worship.

 Rejecting hate speech.

 Assisting victims of violence.

 Encouraging young people to become peacemakers.

 Working together strengthens trust and helps prevent those who seek to divide communities through fear and hatred.

Reflection

Acts of violence committed in the name of religion should not be confused with the authentic teachings of that religion. Every religious community has a responsibility to confront misuse of its sacred texts and to promote interpretations that uphold justice, mercy, and the dignity of human life. The best response to extremism is not greater hatred, but greater commitment to truth, justice, education, and peaceful coexistence.

Conclusion

The Holy Qur'an and the Holy Bible call believers to reject hatred, protect innocent life, pursue justice, and become peacemakers. Extremism, violence against innocent people, and the misuse of religion contradict these shared moral principles.

Christians and Muslims can help build safer and more peaceful societies by standing together against every form of religiously motivated hatred and by promoting compassion, understanding, and lawful justice.

"Do not let the hatred of a people prevent you from being just."

— Qur'an 5:8 "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called children of God."

— Matthew 5:9 True faith is not measured by hatred toward others but by devotion to God expressed through justice, mercy, humility, and love. By rejecting religious extremism and embracing the values taught in their sacred scriptures, Christians and Muslims can become partners in building a future marked by peace, security, and mutual respect.

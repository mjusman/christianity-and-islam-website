Introduction

This bibliography lists important sources consulted or recommended for further study in relation to the themes discussed in Christianity and Islam: A Shared Journey Toward Peace.

The purpose of this bibliography is to guide readers toward primary religious texts, classical sources, academic studies, and works related to interfaith dialogue, peacebuilding, religious ethics, and the relationship between Christianity and Islam.

Readers should approach these sources with respect for the different traditions, scholarly methods, and interpretations represented within them.

I. Primary Religious Sources

The Holy Qur'an

The Qur'an. Various translations and editions.

 The central scripture of Islam, believed by Muslims to be the revealed word of Allah. This book references Qur'anic passages related to peace, justice, mercy, human dignity, religious dialogue, and cooperation. Recommended translations: The Clear Quran, translated by Mustafa Khattab.

 The Noble Qur'an, translated by Muhammad Taqi-ud-Din Al-Hilali  and Muhammad Muhsin Khan.

The Qur'an: A New Translation, translated by Tarif Khalidi.

 The Message of the Qur'an, translated and explained by Muhammad  Asad.

The Holy Bible

The Holy Bible. Various translations and editions.

 The sacred scripture of Christianity, consisting of the Old Testament and the New Testament. This book references Biblical passages concerning peace, love, forgiveness, justice, mercy, and reconciliation. Recommended translations: New International Version (NIV).

 English Standard Version (ESV).

 New Revised Standard Version (NRSV).

 King James Version (KJV).



II. Islamic Classical Sources

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī

Al-Bukhārī, Muhammad ibn Ismāʿīl.

 Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī.

 A major collection of authentic hadith containing reports about the sayings, actions, and teachings of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim

Muslim ibn al-Ḥajjāj.

 Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

 One of the most authoritative collections of hadith in Islamic scholarship.

Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn

Al-Nawawī, Yaḥyā ibn Sharaf.

 Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn (The Gardens of the Righteous).

 A collection of hadith organized around themes including morality, compassion, worship, patience, and good character.

Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr

Ibn Kathīr, Ismāʿīl ibn ʿUmar.

 Tafsīr al-Qur'ān al-ʿAẓīm.

 A classical Qur'anic commentary widely studied in Sunni Islamic scholarship.

Al-Ṭabarī's Tafsīr

Al-Ṭabarī, Muhammad ibn Jarīr.

 Jāmiʿ al-Bayān ʿan Taʾwīl Āy al-Qur'ān.

 One of the earliest and most influential Qur'anic commentaries.

III. Christian Classical and Theological Sources

Early Christian Writings

Augustine of Hippo.

 The City of God.

 A major Christian theological work discussing justice, human society, morality, and the relationship between earthly and divine realities.

Thomas Aquinas

Aquinas, Thomas.

 Summa Theologica.

 A foundational work of Christian theology discussing ethics, law, virtue, and human responsibility.

Early Church Fathers

Selected writings from: Justin Martyr.

 Irenaeus.

 Tertullian.

 Origen.

 John Chrysostom.

 These writings provide historical perspectives on early Christian beliefs and ethical teachings.

Martin Forward

Forward, Martin.

Hans Küng

Küng, Hans.

David B. Burrell

Final Note on Sources

The goal of this bibliography is not to suggest that all listed works share identical viewpoints. Some represent Islamic scholarship, some represent Christian scholarship, and others represent academic approaches to religion and peace studies.

A serious study of Christianity and Islam requires openness to learning, careful reading, respect for different perspectives, and commitment to truth. The pursuit of peace is strengthened when knowledge replaces ignorance, understanding replaces suspicion, and dialogue replaces hostility.
